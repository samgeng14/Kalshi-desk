# Pre-registration: microstructure hypotheses on Kalshi perpetuals

Written 2026-09-11, **before any of the live data has been looked at**, which is the entire point.
The minute-bar study that preceded this one tested 1,736 hypotheses and found nothing above cost;
writing the hypotheses down first is what stops the next study from being 1,736 tests with the
successful ones reported.

Nothing here is tested until the schedule below says so. The $50 account stays paper-only
throughout.

## Data

`scripts/perps_recorder.py`, writing gzipped JSONL to `data/raw/`, partitioned by source and UTC
hour. Sources: Kalshi margin L2 (`orderbook_delta`, `ticker`, `trade` with `last_update_reason`),
CF Benchmarks index at 5 Hz, and Coinbase, Kraken, binance.us and Hyperliquid top of book and
trades.

## Hypotheses

Each is a single pre-committed test. Horizons are 1, 2, 5 and 10 seconds unless stated. The target
is always the **future Kalshi midpoint return**, and every result is reported gross, net of a 10
bps maker/maker round trip, and net of a 17 bps maker/taker round trip.

**H1. Extreme order book imbalance predicts Kalshi midpoint movement over 1 to 10 seconds.**
Test: IC of `L1_OBI`, `L3_OBI`, `L5_OBI`, `L10_OBI` against forward midpoint return, and the
decile spread. Pre-committed direction: positive (imbalance toward the bid predicts the midpoint
rising).

**H2. Microprice minus midpoint predicts 1 to 10 second midpoint movement.**
Test: IC and decile spread of `microprice_minus_mid_bps`. Pre-committed direction: positive. This
is the most likely of the eight to be real and also the most likely to be already in the price.

**H3. Cancel imbalance carries predictive content beyond ordinary order book imbalance.**
Test: the incremental R-squared of `cancel_imbalance` over a model containing `L5_OBI` alone, and
the HAC t on the cancel term in the joint regression. This is the hypothesis that most depends on
`last_update_reason`, which separates a `Trade` from a `Decrease` from each cancel type, and which
most venues do not publish. Pre-committed direction: bids cancelling predicts the midpoint falling.

**H4. Aggressive signed flow predicts continuation over 1 to 10 seconds.**
Test: IC of `trade_imbalance` over 500ms, 1s, 2s and 5s windows. Pre-committed direction:
positive, i.e. continuation, not reversal. Note this is in tension with the one robust minute-bar
finding, which was reversion at one minute in high-flow minutes. If both hold, the sign flips
somewhere between 10 seconds and 1 minute, and locating that crossover is itself the result.

**H5. External-market moves predict subsequent Kalshi movement after controlling for the CF index.**
Test: regress forward Kalshi return on external venue returns **and** CF return jointly; the test
is whether the external coefficients are non-zero given CF. Pre-committed direction: positive.

**H6. CF moves predict Kalshi movement after controlling for external venues.**
The mirror of H5, and the two together decide the shape of the chain. If external survives H5 and
CF fails H6, then external venues lead CF and CF is a lagging republication. If both survive, they
carry separate information.

**H7. The robust 1-minute mean-reversion effect is generated by identifiable short-horizon order
flow dynamics.** Test: condition the minute-bar `revert_1m` effect on the microstructure state at
the start of the minute, and check whether the effect concentrates in identifiable states (high
cancel imbalance, thin replenishment) rather than being uniform. This is the one hypothesis that
connects the negative result to the new data.

**H8. Passive maker quotes around an external/CF fair-value estimate produce positive realized
spread after adverse selection and fees.** The economic hypothesis, and the only one whose failure
would close the whole line of work. Tested last, and only through the fill model, never by
assuming a touch implies a fill.

## Schedule

The reason for a schedule is that looking early and often at accumulating data is how a
pre-registration becomes decoration.

| elapsed | permitted |
|---|---|
| **24 hours** | data-quality report only. Sequence gaps, clock offset, feed uptime, event rates per source, how often each feature is computable. **No hypothesis tests, no distributions, no plots of returns against features.** |
| **72 hours** | exploratory distributions and lead-lag plots. Feature distributions, spread and depth by hour, cross-correlation of external and Kalshi returns by lag. Still **no** hypothesis tests and no decile tables against forward returns. |
| **7 days** | the first pre-registered tests, H1 through H6, run once, all reported whether or not they work. |
| after | H7, then H8 through the fill model. |

## Quote universe, committed in advance

From `research/maker_viability.md`, the contracts whose median quoted gap exceeds the 10 bps
maker/maker round trip: **ADA, WLD, AAVE, VVV, KSHIB, BNB**, plus NEAR when its gap is in the
upper quartile. H8 is tested on these and not on the others.

ADA and WLD are the only two with both width and flow ($1,003 and $502 per minute against gaps of
13.5 and 13.2 bps). If H8 fails on those two it is not rescued by the rest.

BTC and ETH are excluded from H8 by arithmetic, not by judgment: a 0.5 and 1.1 bps quoted gap
cannot pay a 10 bps round trip under any model. They remain in H1 through H7 as predictors and as
the deepest books available for measuring flow.

The correlation between log flow and quoted gap is **−0.64**. There is no contract on this venue
with both a wide spread and heavy flow, so H8 is a bet on width and its failure mode is fill rate.

## Horizons and the accuracy bar, committed in advance

From `research/impulse_viability.md`, computed before any model was fitted:

**BTC and ETH directional tests run at 2m, 5m and 10m only.** The 10s, 30s and 1m horizons are
dropped on arithmetic: 1-minute BTC requires 96.8% directional accuracy at maker/taker, and a
perfect oracle on the top decile of 1-minute BTC moves still loses 3.2 bps after fees. Shorter
windows have smaller moves and cannot do better.

Breakeven directional accuracy on the top 5% of moves, which is the bar these tests must clear:

| contract | h | @17 maker/taker | @10 maker/maker |
|---|---|---|---|
| BTC | 10m | 65.4% | 59.0% |
| ETH | 10m | 61.8% | 57.0% |
| XRP | 10m | 59.7% | 55.7% |

Published short-horizon crypto microstructure models reach roughly 52-56%. **A result below the
maker/maker column is a rejection, not a partial success**, and the threshold is not to be lowered
afterwards to admit one.

Two consequences already committed: passive execution on both legs is part of the strategy rather
than an optimization, because it moves the bar 4 to 6 percentage points and that is the whole
margin; and contracts are ranked by required accuracy rather than by data volume, which puts XRP
and ETH ahead of BTC.

## Analysis rules, committed in advance

- **Multiple testing.** Eight hypotheses across four horizons and however many contracts qualify.
  The family is counted and Benjamini-Hochberg applied at FDR 5%, with Bonferroni also reported.
  The count includes every test run, not every test that worked.
- **Inference.** Newey-West HAC errors and a moving-block bootstrap, as in
  `scripts/alpha_decay.py`. Never a naive t-statistic on overlapping windows.
- **Target.** Future midpoint, never trade price to trade price.
- **Never compute a feature across an order book sequence gap.** The recorder writes a `_gap`
  event naming the expected and received sequence; any window spanning one is dropped, not
  patched.
- **Never forward-fill a stale external price indefinitely.** Every feature row carries the age of
  every feed it used, and a row whose CF or external age exceeds its own horizon is dropped.
- **Report everything.** A hypothesis that fails is written into
  `reports/daily_research_report.md` with the same detail as one that works.

## What would make us stop

If H1 through H6 all fail, or all succeed with net edges below the 10 bps maker/maker hurdle, the
conclusion is that Kalshi perpetuals are not tradable from a laptop at this size, and the right
response is to write that down and stop rather than to add features. The minute-bar study reached
that conclusion at the 1-minute horizon in one day of work, which is the standard to hold this one
to.
