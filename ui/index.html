Perpetual futures loading

move cash $

The margined instance is a separate exchange with its own cash: this tab is the only place on the desk that sees it. A contract is a slice of the coin (KXBTCPERP = 0.0001 BTC), priced in dollars, so P&L is continuous and moves with the coin, not with a settlement. Fees are basis points of notional, on every fill. Funding is paid or received every eight hours by whoever holds the position. The exchange liquidates a position whose margin runs out; the liquidation price of each position is shown next to it. Rails here: order cap, total notional cap, leverage cap and a price band around the mark, all in params.perps. The kill switch and the daily equity stop of the main desk apply too.

Polymarket US loading

The second venue. Same binary contracts as Kalshi, different fees: a taker pays 0.06·p(1−p) a contract, a maker is paid 0.0125·p(1−p). Game markets tick at 0.005 and the books are deep. Every Kalshi game that also trades here gets its fair from this book (strategy xvenue, shown as "polymarket" on the Trades board), and the same game on both venues is a pair: buy the cheap venue, sell the rich one, the spread is locked whatever settles. Rails: order cost and total cost caps, a price band around the mid, the kill switch. Cash here is separate; deposit and withdraw on polymarket.us.

Pair rule off

Every cycle the rule takes the best fresh pair whose locked spread, after both fee schedules, clears min lock and min return: the Kalshi leg first (the thin venue, where the fill is the uncertain part), the Polymarket leg the moment it fills. A Kalshi leg that never fills is pulled after its TTL and the market sits out five minutes. A pair whose Polymarket side moves away before the hedge stays as an unhedged Kalshi position and is flagged. One pair per game; settlement closes both legs.

Rule log

Central risk book

The desk covers risk it considers excessive by itself: anything above the trigger is not risk you chose, it is risk that got away from you. Below the trigger nothing is hedged, because that is the risk you are paid to take. Legs are ordinary orders, so caps, the kill switch and the daily stop still apply, and they only leave without you when AUTO is armed and auto_hedge is in auto mode.

The rest is yours. For each event: what it loses in its worst state, the protection on offer, and what each one costs per dollar of worst case it removes. The desk marks the ones under your price limit and calls what is left after them the optimal remainder - the risk worth keeping, because covering it costs more than it protects. Send what you choose.

Filled positions and possible resting-order fills. Unsent proposals are separate. Risk assumes different markets can lose together until their settlement relationship is verified. Only filled opposite outcomes on the exact same contract receive offset credit; unfilled hedges do not release capacity.

Reduce-position preview

Selling part of the held outcome reduces its directional exposure. Buying its exact opposite has the same payoff effect. This calculator sends no orders.

Quotes may be stale or include your own orders. A buyer and sufficient depth are required. Cancel remaining entry orders before closing so they cannot refill the position. No automatic stops or hedges are active.

When does closing make sense?

There is no minimum holding time that earns profit. Compare the executable exit price with your entry and fees, your current view of the outcome, and your maximum acceptable loss. A 72¢ YES quote is not a 72¢ NO exit. A take-profit is a sale target; a stop-loss is an attempted exit after an adverse move, not a guaranteed fill. If trading has closed, wait for settlement.

Cross-venue model comparison · paper only

Three candidates: complementary spread, residual mean reversion, and venue lead–lag. Entries require a second quote snapshot and depth on both venues. Statistical models need 90+ observations in the same score/period regime and must beat a chronological baseline. A new game event resets the fit.

Paper results assume both quoted legs fill atomically. Real orders have leg risk and latency. This comparison has no live-order route, and does not change your live capital cap or manage manual holdings.
